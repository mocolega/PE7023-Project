\chapter{Conclusions}

This project demonstrated the integrated design of a seven-well subsea production
system capable of sustaining a field-wide target rate of 45,000~STB/d from a
bubble-point reservoir at 3,800~psia.
The two-part scope covered analytical well and pipeline sizing followed by a
Python-based nodal-analysis simulator benchmarked against PIPESIM, and confirmed
that the design objectives are achievable with the selected completion and
surface-facility parameters.

\section{Well and Completion Design}

All seven producers were designed with 3.5-in tubing and sized for individual
plateau rates of approximately 6,400~STB/d.
Gas-lift was selected as the primary artificial-lift mechanism to supplement
reservoir energy as bottomhole flowing pressure declines over the field life.
Sensitivity analysis showed that the combination of tubing size and injection
gas rate provides sufficient operating flexibility to accommodate the expected
range of water-cut increase and GOR evolution without requiring tubing
replacement.
The nodal analysis solutions confirmed stable, unique operating points for each
well at plateau conditions, with bottomhole flowing pressures comfortably above
the bubble point throughout the early production period.

\section{Pipeline and Riser System}

The export pipeline was sized at a 10-in internal diameter over a total tieback
length of 46,617~ft, including a 3,000-ft steel catenary riser, with 2-in
polyurethane insulation applied along the full length.
At plateau, the system delivers fluids to the host facility at an inlet pressure
of approximately 565~psia and an arrival temperature well above the wax
appearance temperature, confirming that flow-assurance requirements are met
under normal operating conditions.
Three principal operational risks were identified for later field life: declining
inlet pressure as water cut rises and reservoir pressure depletes; wax deposition
risk during reduced-rate or shutdown periods when line temperatures approach the
wax appearance temperature; and slug formation at elevated GOR.
Mitigation measures including progressive gas-lift rate adjustment, chemical
inhibitor injection, and pigging capability were incorporated into the recommended
operating envelope.

\section{Python Simulator and PIPESIM Benchmarking}

The object-oriented Python simulator developed in Part~2 reproduced the
PIPESIM mechanistic-model results within acceptable tolerances across the full
range of flow conditions tested.
Pressure-drop predictions along the pipeline agreed to within a few percent
for both the Beggs--Brill and Hagedorn--Brown correlations, validating the
implementation of the multi-phase flow and heat-transfer routines.
Discrepancies at high water-cut conditions were attributed to the simplified
mixture-viscosity treatment in the empirical correlations relative to
PIPESIM's mechanistic model, and represent a clear direction for future
refinement.
The modular code architecture, which separates well inflow, tubing hydraulics,
pipeline hydraulics, and the network solver into distinct classes, facilitates
extension to time-dependent production forecasting and more rigorous
thermodynamic property estimation.

\section{Production Profile and Economic Outlook}

The integrated production profile projects a plateau of 45,000~STB/d sustained
for approximately four years, followed by a natural decline governed by
reservoir depletion and increasing water cut.
Economic screening using the projected oil price and development cost
assumptions yielded a positive net present value, confirming project viability
under the base-case scenario.
The production forecast is sensitive primarily to the rate of water-cut
increase and the cost of gas-lift compression; both parameters warrant
closer definition during front-end engineering.

\section{Recommendations}

Based on the findings of this study, the following work is recommended prior
to a final investment decision:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Acquire PVT fluid samples and conduct laboratory wax-appearance and
          hydrate-equilibrium measurements to replace the estimated
          flow-assurance parameters used in this study.
    \item Perform a full dynamic simulation (e.g., OLGA) of the pipeline and
          riser to quantify slug volumes and confirm the sizing of slug-catcher
          facilities at the host.
    \item Extend the Python nodal-analysis simulator to a time-stepping
          reservoir-coupled model to generate a rigorous field-decline curve
          and refine the economic analysis.
    \item Evaluate alternative lift strategies, in particular electrical
          submersible pumps, for the later field-life period when reservoir
          pressure and GOR conditions move outside the efficient operating
          window of gas-lift.
\end{itemize}
