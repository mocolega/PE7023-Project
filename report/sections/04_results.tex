\section{Wells}
\label{sec:res_wells}
The well design process was developed as described in Section~\ref{subsec:well}.

\emph{PIPESIM} nodal analysis \cite{Schlumberger2023} and the Darcy productivity
index formulation. The gas-lift rate needed to maintain the
production rate of 6,428.57~STB/d was also determined. The final

\section{Pipeline}
\label{sec:res_pipeline}

The subsea flowline connecting manifold~M2 to the LOP follows the 14-point
bathymetric survey of the corridor, spanning 46{,}617~ft with a maximum riser
depth of 3{,}000~ft. A \textbf{10-inch internal diameter} was selected as the
minimum viable size \cite{Schlumberger2023}, yielding an inlet pressure of
1{,}040~psia at the worst-case condition (WC~=~2\%, 7~wells), within the
1{,}200~psia wellhead pressure constraint. The system remains operable under
reduced production, with an inlet pressure of 605~psia when three wells are
offline simultaneously.

The pipeline inlet pressure decreases with increasing water cut, falling to
785~psia at WC~=~95\%, primarily due to the reduction in mixture density and
frictional losses at higher water fractions \cite{BeggsBrill1973}. All watercut
scenarios remain within the WHP constraint without additional intervention.

Thermal analysis confirmed that full 7-well production maintains outlet
temperatures above the WAT of 100\textdegree F across all water cuts.
However, under reduced production (4~wells) with 1~inch of insulation, the
outlet temperature drops to 86.98\textdegree F at WC~=~2\%, below the WAT
and posing a wax deposition risk. A minimum insulation thickness of
\textbf{2~inches} ($k = 0.2$~Btu/(h$\cdot$\textdegree F$\cdot$ft)) is
therefore specified, raising the worst-case outlet temperature to
107.5\textdegree F. The key pipeline design parameters are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:pipeline_summary}.

\section{Production Strategy}

The field can sustain production with up to three wells offline. If it is
necessary to maintain the minimum wellhead pressure under that condition, the
total field liquid production should be reduced to 19,285~STB/d, corresponding
to four wells producing at the maximum liquid rate.
The economic analysis shows that it is more profitable to produce with a reduced
gas-lift injection rate rather than at the maximum allowable rate.
Figures~\ref{optimal_gas_lift_50} and~\ref{optimal_gas_lift_30} show the optimal
gas-lift injection rate for oil prices of \$50/bbl and \$30/bbl, respectively.

\section{Network Simulator}

A network simulator was developed in Python to model the
production of the field. The Hagedorn-Brown correlation \cite{HagedornBrown1965}
was used to model two-phase flow in the wells and the Beggs-Brill correlation
\cite{BeggsBrill1973} was used to model two-phase flow in the pipeline.
The simulator was validated against \emph{PIPESIM} \cite{Schlumberger2023}
results, showing good agreement in predicted flow rates and pressures at the
wells and manifolds. The simulator represents a useful independent tool for
production optimization when commercial software is unavailable.
